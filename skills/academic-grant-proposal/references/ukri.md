# UKRI / EPSRC / Royal Society Proposals

> Facts last verified 2026-11-12 against the UKRI Funding Service page for the EPSRC New Investigator Award (updated 23 Sep 2026), the UKRI R4RI guidance (30 Apr 2026), the UKRI core-section question, resubmission, full-economic-costing, generative-AI and research-data pages, the EPSRC scheme and portfolio pages (standard research, programme grants, network grants, fellowships), the Royal Society URF 2027 scheme notes, and the Royal Society University Research, Dorothy Hodgkin, Industry and Wolfson Fellowship pages. Funder rules change with every work programme — confirm against the current call documents before relying on any limit or amount.

UK Research and Innovation umbrella covers seven research councils. CS/ML proposals primarily go to **EPSRC** (Engineering and Physical Sciences) with smaller roles for **MRC** (Medical), **STFC** (Particle/Astro/HPC), **AHRC** (Arts/Humanities, for digital humanities). Royal Society runs separate fellowship programmes.

## Mechanism Selection

### EPSRC

| Scheme | Eligibility | Budget | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Standard Research Grant** | Anyone eligible to apply to EPSRC; no closing dates | no limit on value | no limit on length |
| **EPSRC Early Independence Fellowship** | Early-career researchers with postdoctoral experience; open only in specific EPSRC research areas | salary + project costs | up to 5 years full-time (10 part-time) |
| **New Investigator Award (NIA)** | First application to EPSRC as project lead; eligibility is set by funding history, not years since PhD | no limit on grant value (EPSRC funds 80% FEC) | no limit on length; typically 1-3 years |
| **Programme Grant** | World-leading research groups; strategic theme; pre-outline, outline, then full proposal | no figure published on the scheme page | up to 6 years |
| **Network grants** | Project lead meeting EPSRC eligibility; a new interdisciplinary network | no limit under FEC (operating and support costs only, no research costs) | no longer than 3 years |
| **Centres for Doctoral Training (CDT)** | Consortia of research organisations, by dedicated call only | set per call (e.g. 2023 UKRI AI CDTs: £117M across 12 centres) | cohorts trained over about 8 years |

Sizes and durations in the table are taken from the EPSRC scheme pages (NIA, standard research, programme grants, network grants); the opportunity page governs. The EPSRC postdoctoral, open and open plus fellowships closed on 12 Dec 2024; EPSRC postdoctoral fellowships now run only in the mathematical sciences. For the NIA, the opportunity page states: "We do not consider years post-PhD or job title to be a marker of career progression. Eligibility is determined on the basis of funding history" (for example, not previously project lead on a single award above £175,000 FEC, and not having supervised postdoctoral staff for more than 12 months cumulatively; read the full list on the opportunity page). The NIA page also says "There is no limit on the value of the grant" and "new investigator awards are typically one to three years in duration", and "We will not accept uninvited resubmissions".

EPSRC's current themes are: advanced materials; artificial intelligence and robotics; clean energy; engineering; future communications systems; healthcare technologies; information and communication technologies; mathematical sciences; physical sciences; quantum technologies; research infrastructure; resilience and security; sustainable manufacturing. For CS/ML, "artificial intelligence and robotics" and "information and communication technologies" are the usual fits; check EPSRC's remit page for the current list.

### Royal Society

| Scheme | Eligibility | Outcome |
|---|---|---|
| **University Research Fellowship (URF)** | Between three and eight years of actual research experience since PhD by the round's closing date (exceptional cases below three years are considered case by case); outstanding early-career | Up to a maximum of £1,907,400 over **eight years**; years 6-8 depend on a mid-fellowship review at the start of year 4 |
| **Dorothy Hodgkin Fellowship (DHF)** | Early-career researchers with a current and ongoing need for flexible support due to personal circumstances (e.g., primary carer, clinically diagnosed health condition); completed PhD with no more than six years of actual research experience since PhD | "Eight-year fellowship with a maximum of £1,907,400 in funding" |
| **Royal Society Industry Fellowship** | PhD-level researchers (early to senior career) in a permanent UK post; academia-industry secondment | up to £168,000; 2 years full-time, or at least 50% part-time over 4 years |
| **Royal Society Wolfson Fellowship** (replaces the archived Wolfson Research Merit Award) | Senior or exceptional mid-career researchers relocating to the UK, nominated by the host institution | up to £300,000 (salary enhancement at most 20% of salary); up to 5 years |

URF is the UK's flagship early-career fellowship — comparable in selectivity to ERC StG. URF figures are from the 2027-round scheme notes (round closed 9 Sep 2026); the 2028 round is expected to open in July 2027, so re-check the new scheme notes (royalsociety.org/grants/university-research/).

### MRC, STFC, BBSRC, NERC, AHRC, ESRC

Same UKRI Funding Service portal. Each has its own remit and application templates; consult council-specific guidance before drafting.

## Funding Service: New Application Format

The **UKRI Funding Service** (launched 2022, rolling rollout) is the current route for UKRI responsive-mode and many other opportunities, including the EPSRC New Investigator Award. It replaces the Je-S-era PDF Case for Support with **structured questions**, each with its own limit. Key points:

- **Limits are set per opportunity** — word counts for some questions and a page limit for an uploaded document in others. Example, EPSRC New Investigator Award: *Vision and Approach* together in one document of no more than **eight sides of A4** (one side may be the project plan/Gantt chart), *Applicant and team capability to deliver* **1,650 words** (including R4RI modules), *Resources and cost justification* **1,000 words**, *Summary* **550 words**. Other sections and other opportunities differ.
- **Scoring and references**: reviewers score each application from 1 (poor) to 6 (exceptional); references count toward the word count of the question they sit in
- **Structured prompts**: each section asks specific questions; answer them directly
- **Multiple separate fields** instead of one Case for Support — apply the same logical structure but address each prompt fully
- **Static PDF preview** generated post-submission — proofread by exporting

**Legacy terms → current equivalents** (use these when a user says the old words): *Case for Support* ≈ the Vision and Approach questions (with Applicant and team capability for the track record); *Justification of Resources / J-aQR* ≈ Resources and cost justification; *Track Record / CV* ≈ the R4RI in Applicant and team capability to deliver; *Pathways to Impact* ≈ impact addressed within vision and approach (see below).

**Always check the current opportunity page** on the UKRI Funding Service before drafting — the prompts and limits evolve.

## Case for Support (legacy PDF format)

For any opportunity that still asks for a PDF Case for Support (legacy Je-S format), and as a logical structure for the Funding Service Vision/Approach questions, a typical structure is:

### Part 1: Previous Track Record (~2 pp)
- PI's prior research arc and major outputs
- Co-Is' relevant track records
- Concrete metrics: papers, citations, software released, patents, prior grants delivered on-time

### Part 2: Description of Proposed Research and Its Context (~6-8 pp)

1. **Background and Vision** (~1.5 pp) — the field's current state, the gap, your hypothesis
2. **National Importance** — how the work supports UK economy, society, science. EPSRC's standard-research page still names "international excellence and national importance" as the main assessment criteria; answer the Funding Service questions as written and show national importance inside Vision and Approach.
3. **Aims and Objectives** (~0.5 pp) — numbered O1, O2, ...
4. **Research Plan / Workpackages** (~3-4 pp) — per WP: aim, methodology, milestones, deliverables, risks
5. **Programme of Work / Timeline** (~0.5 pp) — Gantt
6. **Management** (~0.5 pp) — for multi-investigator grants

### Part 3: Resources

Cross-references the separate Justification of Resources document (do not duplicate).

## Justification of Resources (J-aQR / JoR)

Legacy (Je-S) name; in the Funding Service the equivalent is the *Resources and cost justification* question (NIA: 1,000 words, high-level costs only, justifying the more substantial costs). The legacy standalone document (typically 2 pp) explained and justified every cost line in the budget.

Address per cost category:
- **Directly Incurred Staff** — PDRA salaries, technician time, PI buy-out. Justify why each role is needed and the FTE level.
- **Directly Allocated Staff** — PI/Co-I time at investigator rate (estimated, not invoiced). Justify time commitment matches the work plan.
- **Estates and Indirect Costs** — institutional rate, automatically calculated; brief acknowledgement.
- **Travel and Subsistence** — itemise conferences, fieldwork, collaboration visits with rationale.
- **Equipment** — see the opportunity's equipment guidance. For the EPSRC NIA: equipment of £25,000 to £400,000 per item can be requested; justify any equipment costing more than £25,000 in Resources and cost justification; quotes need not be included in the application, but "retain quotes for equipment costing more than £138,000" as they may be requested post-panel.
- **Other Directly Incurred** — consumables, compute (cloud or HPC time), open-access publication fees.
- **Sub-contracts** — when work is procured from non-academic partners; must be justified vs. doing in-house.

UKRI uses **Full Economic Costing (fEC)**: institutions calculate total economic cost, UKRI pays a fixed 80% of FEC unless the opportunity states otherwise (exceptions exist, e.g. externally contracted social surveys at 100%). Keep this distinction clear when budgeting.

## Pathways to Impact (legacy / Impact Statement)

In the Funding Service there is no separate Pathways to Impact attachment: UKRI states that impact "will be assessed in the following sections: vision, approach, resources and cost justification", so embed impact across them. Address impact across:

- **Academic beneficiaries**: who in the field gains from the outputs (other UK groups, international, students)
- **Economic and societal beneficiaries**: industry partners, end-users, public services, policymakers
- **Engagement plan**: workshops, secondments, public engagement, open-source releases, datasets, standards
- **Industry engagement**: named partners with letters of support strengthen the case

For CS proposals, embed impact via:
- Open-source release plans with named maintainers
- CDT/PhD training contributions
- Industrial partnership commitments (matched effort, secondments)
- Policy engagement (e.g., Centre for Data Ethics, Alan Turing Institute, regulatory bodies)

## EPSRC Specifics

Funding Service opportunities are assessed against the application sections: vision, approach, applicant and team capability to deliver, resources and cost justification, ethics and responsible research and innovation (RRI), plus scheme-specific sections. For example, the NIA adds career development and research organisation support. Write each section to answer its assessor prompts in the opportunity page.

**Legacy (Je-S era) EPSRC criteria**, which you may still meet in older guidance:

1. **Quality**: novelty, importance, relationship to context, timeliness
2. **Importance**: contribution to research area, impact on other areas
3. **Applicant and Partnerships**: track record, expertise, collaborations
4. **Resources and Management**: justification, value for money

EPSRC's standard-research page still names "international excellence and national importance" as the main assessment criteria; show national importance (UK economy, society, capability) inside Vision and Approach and reference EPSRC's themes.

Funding Service applications include Trusted Research and Innovation questions (relevant NSI Act areas, collaborators, whether an export-control licence is needed) and a research-misuse question in the ethics/RRI section; answers feed UKRI due diligence and may affect award terms.

EPSRC reviewers include both academic and industrial reviewers. Write for both audiences:
- Academic: rigour, novelty, evidence
- Industrial: impact, deployability, time-to-application

## Royal Society Fellowships

URF and DHF applications go through the Royal Society's **Flexi-Grant** system (not e-GAP). Distinct features:

- **Eight-year structure**: URF funding is for eight years, with years 6-8 conditional on a mid-fellowship review at the start of year four (DHF is also an eight-year fellowship)
- **Application centres on the PI**, not the project — Track Record section is heavily weighted
- **Research proposal:** a detailed five-year project plan plus an eight-year vision, as a PDF of four sides of A4 (images and references included; text beyond four pages is truncated) or a 2,000-word text field; separate fields for the abstract (400 words), lay summary (250 words) and personal statement (500 words) (URF 2027 scheme notes)
- **Independence**: applicants must demonstrate intellectual independence from PhD/postdoc supervisors. Co-authored papers with supervisors should be explained
- **Host Institution** statement of support from the Head of Department: Concordat for Research Careers, EDI, adequate space and equipment, protected fellowship time (teaching and administration capped at about six hours per term-time teaching week) and a route to a permanent post (fellows are expected to be strong candidates for permanent posts at the end)
- **Multi-stage selection** (URF notes): initial review by at least two panel members → longlist → independent peer review → panel re-assessment → shortlist → interview → outcome

Statement of motivation and career goals weigh substantially. Demonstrate: independence, ambition matching the scheme's prestige, and a 5-10 year research vision beyond the fellowship period.

## Auxiliary Documents

### Tailored CV / Résumé for Researchers and Innovators (R4RI)
UKRI uses **résumé-style CVs** (R4RI) instead of conventional CVs where an opportunity specifies it (in the 'Applicant and team capability to deliver' section). Structure:

R4RI is a flexible narrative CV. UKRI's guidance asks you to "structure your answer into the four module headings"; the word count is specified in the funding opportunity (NIA: 1,150 words for the modules including references, plus up to 500 words for Additions in the 1,650-word capability section). Submit one R4RI for the whole team. Assessors never score individual modules.

1. **Module 1: contributions to the generation of new ideas, tools, methodologies or knowledge** — key outputs such as data sets, software, research and policy publications; how you communicated ideas and results
2. **Module 2: contributions to the development of others and maintenance of effective working relationships** — teaching, workshops, mentoring, leadership in shaping a team's direction
3. **Module 3: contributions to the wider research and innovation community** — positions of responsibility, reviewing, editing, committee work, strategic leadership
4. **Module 4: contributions to broader research or innovation users and audiences** and towards wider societal benefit — public sector, clients, public understanding, policy development
5. **Additional information (optional)** — context such as career breaks, secondments or voluntary work; not for extra skills or outputs, which "will not be assessed"

There is no separate "Personal Details and Qualifications" module in the R4RI guidance. R4RI rewards **diverse, narrative impact** over publication count. Check the opportunity for the exact template and word limits (council variants exist).

### Letters of Support
- Rules differ by opportunity. EPSRC NIA: one PDF, at most 2 sides of A4 per named project partner, none from the host or co-lead organisations; non-compliant attachments lead to rejection
- Industry partners: matched effort, in-kind contributions, advisory roles
- Academic collaborators: data/sample/instrument access
- International collaborators: scope of partnership

### Data Management Plan (DMP)
UKRI applicants will normally be asked for an outline of how they plan to manage and share research data; a full project data management plan building on that outline must be in place within six months of the start of the award. Council policies apply until the new UKRI research data policy takes effect for opportunities launched on or after 1 April 2027. The DCC checklist is a useful template: types of data generated, metadata standards, sharing mechanism, repository, retention period, ethical/legal restrictions.

## Submission Logistics

- **Portal:** UKRI Funding Service (https://funding-service.ukri.org/)
- **Royal Society (URF, DHF):** Flexi-Grant; check each scheme page
- **Generative AI (UKRI policy):** declare substantive use (minimal use such as language polishing need not be declared); never generate whole sections without human involvement; no generative AI during interviews. The Royal Society asks you to name the AI source and how content was generated.
- **Institutional sign-off**: Research Office must approve before submission. Allow ≥2 weeks
- **Costing tool**: institution's TRAC/fEC tool generates budgets — supply work plan to research office early
- **Common rejection reasons:**
  - National Importance missing or weak (EPSRC)
  - Track Record does not demonstrate independence (URF, NIA)
  - JoR does not justify each line, or contradicts the work plan
  - Impact embedded too thinly throughout — reviewers cannot find the engagement plan
  - Risk register absent or generic
  - Funding Service: each question has its own word limit (references count toward it); stay inside the limit and check the PDF preview; sections answered only partially
- **Resubmission:** UKRI policy: only invited resubmissions are accepted (uninvited resubmissions are rejected regardless of time elapsed); the policy does not apply to fellowships. A substantially different programme of work counts as a new submission. The EPSRC NIA page says "We will not accept uninvited resubmissions of projects that have been submitted to UKRI or any other funder". The Royal Society URF allows reapplication while you remain eligible.

## CS / Systems / ML Notes for UK Funders

- **Alan Turing Institute** affiliations and EPSRC AI Hubs add weight when relevant
- Past or periodic UKRI/EPSRC themed investments in this area have included a Trustworthy Autonomous Systems Hub, AI for Net Zero and Responsible AI UK. These are examples only, not current open calls: check the current funding opportunities on the UKRI site and apply only to a call that is open
- **DSIT** (Department for Science, Innovation and Technology) AI strategy alignment scores; reference the UK AI Safety Institute or AI Opportunities Action Plan when relevant
- Compute justification: **JADE2**, **Isambard-AI**, **Bristol AI Centre**, **Tier-1 HPC** (Archer2) access available — name in Facilities
- Impact case studies for CS proposals: open-source release with adoption metrics, datasets contributed, standards influenced
